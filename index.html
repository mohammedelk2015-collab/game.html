A NEW CHAPTER IN MOROCCAN HISTORY

Understanding October 31, the national holiday known as Aid Al Wahda, and its importance to Morocco.

SPECIAL REPORT • OCTOBER 31 • MOROCCO

Morocco — A special edition celebrating national unity.

🇲🇦 What Is Unity Day?

Unity Day is a Moroccan national holiday known in Arabic as Aid Al Wahda (عيد الوحدة). It is celebrated every year on October 31. The holiday celebrates Morocco's national unity, identity and territorial integrity, particularly in connection with the Western Sahara issue.

🇲🇦 Who Created It?

King Mohammed VI

King Mohammed VI established Unity Day and gave it its official name, Aid Al Wahda. The Royal Cabinet announced the decision on November 4, 2025.

🇲🇦 Why Was It Made?

Morocco created Unity Day to celebrate national unity and mark what the country considers an important development in the Western Sahara issue. It also represents Moroccan identity, history and territorial integrity.

📅 When Was It Made?

October 31 is the date chosen for the annual celebration. The first celebration took place in 2025, while the official announcement establishing the holiday was made on November 4, 2025.

Next celebration: October 31, 2026.

📍 Where Was It Established?

Unity Day was officially established through an announcement by the Royal Cabinet in Rabat, the capital city of Morocco, on November 4, 2025, under the authority of King Mohammed VI. Rabat is an important political centre of Morocco and is home to the Royal Palace and central government institutions.

🌍 Why Was October 31 Chosen?

October 31, 2025, was an important date because the United Nations Security Council adopted Resolution 2797 concerning Western Sahara. The resolution supported negotiations based on Morocco's autonomy proposal as a framework for reaching a political solution. King Mohammed VI also delivered an important speech to the Moroccan people on that day.

📖 The Western Sahara Issue

Western Sahara is a territory in northwestern Africa whose political status has been disputed for decades. Morocco considers it part of its territory, while the Polisario Front seeks independence. The United Nations continues to work towards a political solution.

🚶 The Green March

The Green March took place on November 6, 1975. It involved a large mobilisation of Moroccan civilians towards Western Sahara and remains an important event in Morocco's modern history.

📰 A Longer Look at Unity Day

Morocco's Unity Day, known in Arabic as Aid Al Wahda (عيد الوحدة), is an important national holiday celebrated every year on October 31 in the Kingdom of Morocco. It was established by King Mohammed VI in 2025 and officially announced on November 4, 2025, through a statement from the Royal Cabinet in Rabat, Morocco.

The date October 31 was chosen because, on that day in 2025, the United Nations Security Council adopted Resolution 2797 concerning Western Sahara, supporting negotiations based on Morocco's autonomy proposal as a framework for reaching a political solution. King Mohammed VI also delivered an important speech to the Moroccan people on that date, describing the developments as a historic moment for the country.

Unity Day was created to celebrate Morocco's national unity, territorial integrity, history, culture and identity, and to recognise the importance of bringing Moroccan people together regardless of their region or background. It is also an opportunity for younger generations to learn about Morocco's history, remember important historical events such as the Green March of November 6, 1975, and the country's independence in 1956, and understand the importance of peace, cooperation, stability and development.

The holiday represents Morocco's official position concerning Western Sahara, although the territory's international political status remains disputed. Every year on October 31, Moroccans can use this occasion to remember their shared history, celebrate their national identity, learn about their country's past and look forward to its future.
